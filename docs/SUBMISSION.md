# Submission text

## One-liner

RECALL: a training-free memory that lets an open world model remember what it already generated, so a scene is still there when the camera looks back.

## Short description

World models forget. LingBot-World2 keeps only about 4.5 seconds of context, so when the camera turns away and comes back, it invents a different scene. RECALL archives the model's own earlier frames, finds the ones that match where the camera is going, and splices them back into its attention. No training, no weight changes. On 20 unseen PlayWorld scenes, judged consistency after a look-away rose by +0.77 [+0.51, +1.04].

## Full description

**Problem.** Track 4 asks how long a generated world can hold together while an agent plays in it. We start from LingBot-World2 14B, the strongest open model, and fix the failure PlayWorld's judges see most: when the camera looks away and returns, the room, signs and objects are different.

**What we built.** RECALL is an episodic memory for the model's attention. After each chunk it saves the frames' cached keys and values with the camera pose. When the camera comes back, it retrieves the 10 nearest frames by pose and splices them into the attention window (first 6, retrieved 10, most recent 8). We also found that storing only the frame and rebuilding its values on demand takes 6 MB per frame instead of 1.2 GB, about 200x less.

**Results.**
- We reproduced the open baseline on all 171 PlayWorld tasks, twice: 1.85 and 1.81 against the published 1.82.
- On a pre-registered revisit test (20 unseen PlayWorld scenes, the camera away for 0, 6, 12 and 24 seconds, PlayWorld's own judge and rubric), RECALL improves judged consistency at every length: +0.85, +1.31, +0.66 and +0.34, +0.77 pooled [+0.51, +1.04].
- A pilot on 8 scenes at 24 seconds suggests that removing the 24-frame archive cap helps further (1.64 to 2.69).
- On PlayWorld's own 171 tasks the score matches the released model (1.80 to 1.86 against 1.82), because most of those episodes are shorter than the model's own memory.

**Autonomy.** The research ran as an autoresearch loop: AI agents proposed methods, ran experiments on cloud GPUs, audited each other's code and results, and rebuilt what failed. Over two days it tried 17 ideas, kept 4, found partial results for 5 and dropped 8. A human set the goals and the budget. Findings are recorded in a research memory.

**Multi-agent extension.** We reused the rollout, camera conditioning, event-caption director and warm runtime to produce two labeled first-person LingBot videos from one scripted event plan. The synchronized tiles make cross-view disagreements visible. This is an exploratory prototype: the basket handoff remains unresolved and the visual histories are independent; the single-camera benchmark gains are not multi-agent results.

**Reproducibility.** Every run has a pre-registered criterion and a frozen, committed configuration. Per-episode scores, judge outputs and logs are in the repo, the leaderboard is generated by a script, and negative results are kept.
